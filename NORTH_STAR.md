# NORTH STAR — strategic decision lock

## Measurement lock

The strategic gate is **Decision Frequency**: the number of distinct decision days per eligible family over a 30-day period, where a decision day contains at least one `SAVE`, `PLAN_ADD`, or `CTA_CLICK`; `DETAIL_OPEN` is explicitly excluded. The eligible cohort is limited to families registered at least 60 days earlier and with at least one prior meaningful action, so one-off SEO visitors do not define the metric. Popularity, distance, and price must be treated as baselines/constraints rather than taste signals in any portability test. Results from September–December 2026 must be marked as seasonally favorable and must not be silently generalized to February or other low-season periods. Before interpreting the gate, we first verify that the eligible cohort is large enough to produce a stable estimate; if it is not, the autumn objective becomes cohort/data acquisition, not reinterpretation of the threshold.

## Branch A — Decision Frequency ≥ 1.5

If the measured Decision Frequency is **≥ 1.5 decision days per eligible family per month**, mamaGo treats repeated family leisure choice as sufficiently frequent to justify the Family Decision Engine hypothesis. We proceed to validate taste portability against a non-personalized popularity baseline, using a manually labeled Universal Experience Attribute Schema on Minsk objects before building parsers, provenance infrastructure, cross-city ingestion, or ML ranking. Only if the attribute model beats the popularity baseline after distance and price are controlled do we invest in automated city bootstrap and cross-city personalization. The target company remains a global consumer product built around recurring family decisions, with local content and B2B supply serving the decision engine rather than defining the business.

## Branch B — Decision Frequency < 1.5

If the measured Decision Frequency is **< 1.5**, including values such as **1.2**, we reject the Family Decision Engine as the primary venture-scale thesis for mamaGo; we do not explain the miss away with seasonality, UX debt, small product imperfections, or the argument that the metric was "almost" reached. We stop investment in universal attribute infrastructure, new-city parsers, travel mode, and sophisticated personalized ranking beyond what is justified for the existing local product. mamaGo may continue as a lean Minsk discovery/B2B asset where economically sensible, but because the previously estimated local marketplace ceiling is not compatible with the desired company scale, the strategic work shifts to finding and testing a different, genuinely higher-frequency family job-to-be-done rather than spending another year trying to force leisure discovery into a Flo-like habit loop.
